The king's field guide

Strength needs a leader.

You begin alone. Every ape you free can become part of your horde. Protect your crown, preserve your tribe, and make the forest yours.

Light means danger

Flashlights and tower beams reveal any ape they touch. Keep to shadows and trees. Humans shout, run to alarms, and call reinforcements by radio. Interrupt a caller before help arrives. Break sight and move away from the last place you were seen.

Free your kin

Captive apes wait in cages around human camps. Strike a cage until it breaks, then call with Q to gather the freed apes. A large horde can overwhelm a patrol, but a crowded road is easy to spot. Hold your tribe in safe cover and scout alone when the hunt is hot.

Give your tribe a home

Settle on safe open land near berry bushes. Builders spend timber to expand the lodge and build huts around it. Each hut shelters apes and has its own health: human raiders and explosives can damage or destroy it. Defend your homes; after an attack, builders spend timber to repair and rebuild. Occupied settlements keep raising young until their completed homes are full. More finished buildings and adult residents make growth faster; gardens feed the growing village. Young apes grow up in 35 seconds. Your tribe can reach 1,000 living apes, including residents and young. Open the map to choose work priorities and deliver food. Choose Find Settlement or press V to show an edge arrow for every settlement, including homes whose residents have joined your horde, with distance and attack warnings.

Turn the hunt against them

Healthy adult apes can take a few ordinary hits, but concentrated gunfire still tears through a tribe. Use cover, then strike together. Military facilities hold large groups of captives; liberating them also wins food supplies. Your king slowly recovers when safe. Later marksmen can kill an ape in one shot: watch their aiming line and move away from its endpoint. Orange circles warn of grenades. Flank shield guards or swarm them. Medics support nearby soldiers. At 120 followers, mechanized squads hunt your army. At 200, military mobilization unlocks tanks. Leave the cannon’s warning circle before the shell arrives. Tanks resist frontal attacks: flank their rear or swarm the hull to disable the turret. Ambush APCs and troop trucks before their soldiers dismount. Larger bases have layered barricades; strike radios, fuel, barracks and depots to weaken the regional response. Forests remain your shelter.

Commission your village

Stand beside the main hut and choose Build or press B. The menu pauses the world while you choose. Pay village timber and food to commission spear towers, training facilities, defenses, housing, gardens, stores, and workshops. Builders clear spacious plots and finish each structure; work pauses during an attack. Staffed spear towers launch visible spears up to 900 world units, beyond enemy gun range. Larger villages unlock more building slots. Staffed training facilities gradually improve residents' health and strikes, and their training stays with them when recruited.

A liberation campaign

Open the map to form divisions around champions or trained veterans and specialize your villages. New military territory holds regional prisons, blacksites, transfer fortresses and quarry camps. Cut power or destroy internal release controls before breaching locked cells. Escort the freed crowd through an opening and beyond the walls; guards can recapture isolated survivors. Take injured elites to a safe settlement to recover, and station builders or wardens where their people need them. Fog and rain help infiltration. Background music plays quietly during the campaign; its volume and mute are in Settings.

Let your tribe use its strengths

Bold apes favor front-line troops and armor. Guardians defend the king and their village. Saboteurs break radios, alarms, depots, and defenses. Rescuers seek captive cages; skirmishers target snipers, officers, and medics. Spread charges give them room to flank. Adult capuchins, gibbons, and chimpanzees climb the marked vines and scaffolds on fortress walls. They can fight wall guards, drop inside and sabotage gate controls. Heavy apes wait for a gate or breach. Capuchins throw from range; mandrills use Rally Roar to briefly speed up nearby allies. Prepare gorilla and orangutan log shields from village timber or fallen trees, then advance with the carriers in front. Keep striking when beside a wall to break it and open a route for the rest of the tribe.

Plan a flank, then attack

Select species with the ape pictures or 1–6; 0 selects all followers and Shift combines species. Tap one or more ground points, enemies or discovered gate controls in order. Press Enter or the triangle to go; Shift+Enter or + appends to existing orders. Backspace removes the last draft point. Aim the pointer and tap E to charge at the nearest target ahead; hold E to charge at humans and vehicles, including tanks, while ignoring structures. Both use the same long range and continue forward when no target is available. H shield advance, J sabotage, K climb, L Rally Roar, U prepare logs. Choose the individual-ape command (I) to pick one follower. Drag the battlefield while a group is selected to pan, and wheel or pinch to zoom. O or the clickable Cancel button discards unissued waypoints and returns control to the King; issued orders continue. Escape always pauses. Right-click the final point to send the route with an area attack: earlier waypoints happen first, then the group attacks nearby troops, vehicles and enemy structures. On touch, choose Finish with attack (P on PC), then tap the final point. This attack is always the last stop; use Go to replace it with a new route. The expanded command dock includes four saved species groups: hold A–D to save, tap to recall; PC also supports Ctrl+Shift+1–4 to save and Ctrl+1–4 to recall.

Read the late-war weapons

Breach troopers close in with shotguns; forward observers illuminate confirmed positions; volley grenadiers throw paired explosives; rotary gunners must spin up before firing. Twinfang tanks fire three shells at one warned point. Thunderback tanks bombard three marked impact zones. Cyclone tanks sweep a narrow firing lane, then cool down. Move out of the warning lane or behind cover, and flank armor while its weapon rests. Gatling Redoubts, Artillery Bastions and Iron Citadels appear in newly explored military regions. Their gun nests face outward: infiltrate from behind, destroy a nest, or break the lightning-marked power relay inside to silence its connected guns.

Earn your crown

At 100 living apes across your kingdom, become King of the Jungle. At 300, become the Warlord. Each milestone pauses the world for a ceremony, changes your crown and soundtrack, and unlocks new construction and equipment. Purchase the Royal lodge and Warlord citadel expansions at a main hut to open larger housing districts and up to three palisade rings. Your apes and King automatically climb friendly palisades. Visit a completed workshop and press B to commission individual spears, wooden fists, climber torches, or King armor. Only one recipient is equipped at a time; keep the King beside the workshop while his armor is fitted. Torches light your surroundings but reveal the bearer. Titles remain earned after losses.

Playing by touch

Use the left stick to move and the right button to strike. Tap the arrow-in-a-circle at the right of the species row to expand commands. Select species, tap successive destinations or targets, then tap the triangle to send them. Army taps never strike with the King. Pinch to zoom and drag to pan while a group is selected. Choose Charge, Charge nearest target, Charge troops & vehicles or Spread charge, then tap the ground to send the horde in that direction. Tap the R recall arrow for nearby field apes; hold it for all field apes. Tap the T circled recall arrow for all field apes; hold it until the progress bar fills to include settlement residents. Tap Build beside the main hut to commission village structures. Tap Find Settlement to follow the edge arrows home; tap again to hide them.
