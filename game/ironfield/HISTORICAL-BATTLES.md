# Historical battle collection

Thirty playable, sourced scenarios span 490 BCE to 1815 CE. Each uses an explicit opening deployment, named army sectors, terrain, flank forces, and reserves rather than the generic roster auto-placement.

These are representative game adaptations. Troop totals and distances are compressed independently for playability; they are not a single numerical scale, a claim of exact force ratios, or a surveyed map. Historical sources describe the arrangement and tactical situation. The game’s weapon ranges, damage, armor, movement, and outcomes remain game rules. Some battlefield locations and precise deployments are disputed.

## Reading the battlefield

The schematic fields rotate the principal armies toward one another for readable play. Left and right in formation names refer to the historical army, not necessarily the top of the screen. The settings use familiar IRONFIELD archetypes for the nearest troop role: hoplites for ancient heavy spear infantry, swordsmen for several kinds of close infantry, pikes for sarissa or schiltron blocks, lancers for mounted shock troops, and musketeers for firearm infantry. Generic artwork and statistics do not imply identical historical equipment.

Musketeers follow IRONFIELD’s three-rank game drill, including in battles whose actual armies used other depths. A three-file column depicts a marching body; it does not claim all historical columns were only three files wide. Bodies placed with explicit offsets retain the intended relative depth and frontage.

Historical outcomes are not forced. Reinforcement arrival schedules, command failures, ammunition shortages, changing allegiance, weather, and every phase of a multi-hour battle are not simulated. Battle-specific limits appear in the collection menu. Players can reproduce later decisions through direct orders.

## Sources and scenario notes

Sources consulted 4 November 2026. The public links are also available beside each battle in the historical menu.

### Thermopylae · 480 BCE

Malis, Greece · Ancient.

Greek heavy foot occupy the pass with supporting contingents behind them. Persian assault bodies wait in separate depths outside the constricted entrance, with missile troops supporting the approach.

Equipment, armor, and movement use IRONFIELD troop archetypes rather than exact period statistics.

The opening pass defense is represented, including allied Greeks. The later mountain-path encirclement and final stand are not scripted; the pass is widened for playable body clearance.

- [Herodotus: Histories Book VII · Fordham University](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/ancient/herodotus-thermopylae.asp)

### Hydaspes · 326 BCE

Punjab, South Asia · Ancient.

Porus places elephants in front of his infantry, with cavalry on both flanks. Macedonian pikes and light troops approach the center while a mounted striking force occupies the right.

Pikes and cavalry represent Macedonian roles; elephant howdahs and all armor use generic game artwork. Indian chariots have no direct roster equivalent and are omitted.

This begins after Alexander’s crossing. River ferries, prior diversions, and the precise historical elephant spacing are outside the scenario.

- [Arrian: Anabasis V.15 · public-domain translation](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Anabasis_of_Alexander/Book_V/Chapter_XV)

### Zama · 202 BCE

North Africa · Ancient.

Roman companies stand in aligned passages rather than a closed checkerboard, with Laelius on the left and Massinissa on the right. Carthaginian elephants lead mercenaries, native infantry, and a distant veteran reserve.

Swordsmen stand for Roman and Carthaginian foot. Cavalry approximate Numidian light horse without adding bows. The simulator does not impose historical elephant panic.



- [Polybius: Histories XV.9–16 · University of Chicago](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Polybius/15%2A.html)

### Stirling Bridge · 1297 CE

Stirling, Scotland · Medieval.

The Scots occupy rising ground near the bridgehead. A selected English vanguard has already crossed, while further infantry and horse stand on the far bank behind a single crossing.

Equipment, armor, and movement use IRONFIELD troop archetypes rather than exact period statistics.

The river loop is simplified and the crossing widened for practical navigation. The bridge does not collapse; neither timed crossings nor the historical cut-off are forced.

- [Stirling Bridge battlefield inventory · Historic Environment Scotland](https://portal.historicenvironment.scot/apex/f?p=1505%3A300%3A%3A%3A%3A%3AVIEWTYPE%2CVIEWREF%3Adesignation%2CBTL28)

### Flodden · 1513 CE

Northumberland, England · Pike & shot.

Huntly and Home occupy the Scottish left, James the center, Lennox and Argyll the right, with Bothwell in reserve. English divisions oppose the hill while Stanley’s wing is offset to threaten the Scottish right.

Halberdiers approximate English bills; generic pikes represent Scottish continental-length weapons. Equipment and gun effects remain game rules.



- [Flodden battlefield report · Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/content/docs/listing/battlefields/flodden/)

### Pinkie Cleugh · 1547 CE

Musselburgh, Scotland · Pike & shot.

English foot form three supporting battle sectors with shot, bowmen, artillery, and mounted wings. Scottish strength lies in deep pike divisions, backed by a smaller missile element.

Equipment, armor, and movement use IRONFIELD troop archetypes rather than exact period statistics.

The land battle is represented; English naval support is omitted. Pike depths and frontage are compressed, and firearms follow the game’s three-rank drill.

- [Pinkie battlefield inventory · Historic Environment Scotland](https://portal.historicenvironment.scot/apex/f?p=1505%3A300%3A%3A%3A%3A%3AVIEWTYPE%2CVIEWREF%3Adesignation%2CBTL15)

### Trenton · 1776 CE

New Jersey, North America · Musket age.

One American body approaches the town from the west while Greene’s body and Washington approach from the north. Hessian regiments assemble within the open street sectors, with Assunpink Creek behind the town.

Equipment, armor, and movement use IRONFIELD troop archetypes rather than exact period statistics.

The scenario begins with the garrison forming. Surprise, fatigue, building interiors, and the earlier Delaware crossing are not scripted.

- [Battle of Trenton · George Washington’s Mount Vernon](https://www.mountvernon.org/library/digitalhistory/digital-encyclopedia/article/battle-of-trenton)

### Princeton · 1777 CE

New Jersey, North America · Musket age.

A forward American detachment confronts the British 17th Foot near the orchard. Additional American infantry and Moulder’s battery stand farther back, while British supporting bodies occupy a second depth.

Equipment, armor, and movement use IRONFIELD troop archetypes rather than exact period statistics.

Reinforcements are present from the opening rather than arriving on a clock. Rifle and bayonet differences use generic musket infantry rules.

- [Princeton Battlefield State Park · New Jersey DEP](https://dep.nj.gov/parksandforests/state-park/princeton-battlefield-state-park/)
- [Moulder’s Battery battlefield interpretation · Borough of Princeton / NJ State Parks](https://www.princetonnj.gov/DocumentCenter/View/15452/Battle-Window-at-Moulder-s-Battery_submission-for-ABPP-Rev-and-Princeton-Sec-106-Rvw_122023-PDF)

### Austerlitz · 1805 CE

Moravia, Central Europe · Napoleonic.

A strong French center stands opposite the Pratzen plateau while the right wing is comparatively thin. Allied strength concentrates in southern columns, leaving a smaller central force and a separate northern wing.

Equipment, armor, and movement use IRONFIELD troop archetypes rather than exact period statistics.

The scenario depicts the developing central opportunity. Mist, timed arrivals, frozen ponds, and each prior maneuver are not simulated.

- [The Battle of Austerlitz and the Principles of War · Fondation Napoléon](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/the-battle-of-austerlitz-and-the-principles-of-war)

### Borodino · 1812 CE

Western Russia · Napoleonic.

Russian foot hold successive sectors behind the central redoubt and southern fieldworks, with a northern village sector and reserves. French infantry faces the works with supporting cavalry and concentrated gun batteries.

Trenches and short rectangular earthwork panels approximate the redoubt and flèches, whose exact angular geometry is not reproduced. Musket and gun effects are game balance.



- [Napoleon’s Russian campaign: Borodino · Fondation Napoléon](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/timelines/napoleons-russian-campaign-from-the-niemen-to-moscow)
- [Contemporary Borodino map · Service historique de la Défense](https://www.servicehistorique.sga.defense.gouv.fr/les-tresors-du-shd/carte-de-la-bataille-de-borodino)

### Marathon · 490 BCE

Attica, Greece · Ancient.

Athens and Plataea occupy a continuous front, with shallow central ranks and deeper wings. Persian strength is concentrated opposite the center; marsh and coastal water bound the field.

Hoplites represent Greek heavy foot; Persian infantry and archers use light-foot archetypes. No Persian cavalry is placed: its role in this battle is uncertain.



- [Herodotus: Marathon · University of Texas at Arlington](https://websites.uta.edu/hunnicut/reading-list/readings-western-civilization/herodotus-the-battle-of-marathon/)

### Leuctra · 371 BCE

Boeotia, Greece · Ancient.

The Theban left is much deeper than its center and refused right. The Spartan king stands opposite that concentration. Both sides begin with horse in front of their hoplites.

Equipment, armor, and movement use IRONFIELD troop archetypes rather than exact period statistics.

The famous fifty-shield depth is represented by a proportionally deep block, not fifty full-size ranks.

- [Xenophon: Hellenica VI.4 · Fordham University](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/ancient/371leuctra.asp)

### Gaugamela · 331 BCE

Northern Mesopotamia · Ancient.

The Macedonian phalanx holds the center, the Companions the right, and Parmenion the left. Persian cavalry extends both wings and Darius commands from the center.

Pikemen stand for the sarissa phalanx, lancers for Companions, hoplites for Greek mercenaries. Scythed chariots are omitted because the roster has no equivalent.



- [Arrian: Alexander at Gaugamela · University of Texas at Arlington](https://websites.uta.edu/hunnicut/reading-list/readings-western-civilization/arrian-alexander-at-the-battle-of-gaugamela/)

### Cannae · 216 BCE

Apulia, Italy · Ancient.

Iberian and Gallic companies form a forward-bowed center. African veterans wait back on the wings, with cavalry outside them. Rome concentrates deep infantry between its cavalry flanks.

Swordsmen represent legionaries and Iberian/Gallic infantry; hoplites represent African heavy foot. Numidian light cavalry use the cavalry archetype, without invented horse archers.



- [Polybius: Histories III.107–118 · Fordham University](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/ancient/polybius-cannae.asp)

### Hastings · 1066 CE

Sussex, England · Medieval.

English foot occupy the hill in a joined shield wall. Norman missile troops lead, with infantry behind and mounted troops farther back across three sectors.

Swordsmen and halberdiers approximate English shield bearers and axe-armed housecarls; poleaxes and cavalry equipment are generic visual stand-ins.



- [What happened at Hastings · English Heritage](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/1066-battle-of-hastings-abbey-and-battlefield/history-and-stories/what-happened-battle-hastings)

### Bannockburn · 1314 CE

Stirling, Scotland · Medieval.

Three Scottish schiltron blocks lead with a reserve behind. English cavalry occupy the forward space, with infantry crowded farther back between constrained flanks.

Pike blocks stand for schiltrons. The game cannot reproduce every hedge, bog, or the historical English command failure.

This is a schematic second-day arrangement; the precise location and layout remain debated.

- [Bannockburn battlefield inventory · Historic Environment Scotland](https://portal.historicenvironment.scot/apex/f?p=1505%3A300%3A%3A%3A%3A%3AVIEWTYPE%2CVIEWREF%3Adesignation%2CBTL4)

### Agincourt · 1415 CE

Artois, France · Medieval.

English men-at-arms occupy the center, with longbow companies on either side. French foot stand in successive deep bodies, with mounted detachments at the flanks. Woods compress the frontage.

Equipment, armor, and movement use IRONFIELD troop archetypes rather than exact period statistics.

Archer-wing placement is a conventional reconstruction; exact dispositions and the battlefield site are disputed. Stakes and mud are not separately simulated.

- [The battle of Agincourt · College of Arms](https://www.college-of-arms.gov.uk/news-grants/news/item/119-the-battle-of-agincourt)

### Bosworth · 1485 CE

Leicestershire, England · Medieval.

Oxford’s compact vanguard shields Henry’s reserve against the Royal front. Richard’s mounted household sits behind his center, with the Stanley contingent represented at the Tudor flank.

Equipment, armor, and movement use IRONFIELD troop archetypes rather than exact period statistics.

Deployments are conjectural. The two-team engine places the Stanleys with Henry from the start and cannot reproduce their delayed commitment or Northumberland’s inactivity.

- [Bosworth registered battlefield · Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1000004?section=official-list-entry)

### Naseby · 1645 CE

Northamptonshire, England · Pike & shot.

Both armies place foot in the center and cavalry on each wing. Cromwell leads Parliament’s right, Rupert the opposing Royalist right. A dismounted musket detachment represents the hedgerow dragoons.

Musketeers use the game’s three-rank drill instead of exact Civil War depths. Pike and shot are separate neighboring companies; dragoons begin dismounted.



- [Battle of Naseby · National Army Museum](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/battle-naseby)

### Blenheim · 1704 CE

Bavaria, Germany · Musket age.

French and Bavarian troops anchor on Blenheim, Oberglau, and Lutzingen, with weaker gaps between. Marlborough’s wing and Eugene’s wing begin opposite those sectors; the Danube closes one flank.

Woodland cover stands in for village clusters; the shallow Nebel is represented by several open crossings. The map is a schematic sector layout.



- [Battle of Blenheim · National Army Museum](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/battle-blenheim)

### Dettingen · 1743 CE

Main Valley, Germany · Musket age.

Both armies have two central infantry lines and horse on the wings. French guns fire from beyond the Main, while Gramont’s troops have left the ravine behind their battle line.

Equipment, armor, and movement use IRONFIELD troop archetypes rather than exact period statistics.

### Bunker Hill · 1775 CE

Massachusetts, North America · Musket age.

One Provincial body holds the hill redoubt sector while another extends toward the Mystic shore. British battalions prepare an uphill attack and a separate shoreward approach.

Hill and sparse wall sections approximate earthworks and rail fences. The game does not model the defenders’ historical ammunition shortage.



- [The Battle of Bunker Hill · National Park Service](https://home.nps.gov/articles/000/the-battle-of-bunker-hill.htm)

### Freeman’s Farm · 1777 CE

New York, North America · Musket age.

The Crown advances in three separated sectors, with Hamilton in the center, Fraser inland, and Riedesel by the Hudson. American detachments occupy the central and inland woodland approaches.

Loose-order musketeers represent Morgan’s riflemen, without separate rifle ballistics.

Three approach sectors are present together; reinforcements and their historical arrival times are not scripted.

- [Battle of Freeman’s Farm · National Park Service](https://home.nps.gov/sara/learn/historyculture/battle-of-freemans-farm.htm)

### Cowpens · 1781 CE

South Carolina, North America · Musket age.

Three American infantry positions stand one behind another, with cavalry behind a low hill. British foot and light guns lead their advance, with dragoon reserves on a wing.

All firearm infantry use musketeers. Differences in militia training, rifle range, and ammunition are not individually represented.

Militia withdrawal after two volleys must be ordered by the player; the scenario does not force the historical sequence.

- [The Battle of Cowpens · National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/cowp/learn/historyculture/the-battle-of-cowpens.htm)

### Guilford Courthouse · 1781 CE

North Carolina, North America · Musket age.

North Carolina militia occupy the first position, Virginia militia the second, and Continentals the third. American detachments guard both flanks; British foot form a broad advancing front.

Militia and Continentals share musket statistics. Woodland and spacing represent the layered defense; historical withdrawals are left to battlefield orders.

### Yorktown · 1781 CE

Virginia, North America · Musket age.

### Waterloo · 1815 CE

Belgium · Napoleonic.

Allied infantry hold behind the Mont-Saint-Jean ridge, with forward detachments representing Hougoumont, La Haye Sainte, and Papelotte. French infantry, guns, and cavalry occupy successive depths opposite the ridge.

Generic musketeers use three ranks instead of varied historical depths; hill cover approximates the reverse slope, and woodland patches stand in for farm strongpoints.

Opening deployments only: Prussian arrivals and later cavalry-versus-square phases are not scripted.

- [Battle of Waterloo · National Army Museum](https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/battle-waterloo)

## Verification

Run `node tests/historical-battles-test.cjs` from this game directory. The checks verify unique IDs, source metadata, troop totals, explicit formation coverage, named sectors, valid formations, finite starting bodies, map bounds, terrain clearance, and physical separation for all thirty encounters. Separate layout checks verify Marathon’s stronger wings, Leuctra’s deep Theban left, Cannae’s projecting center, Cowpens’s three positions, and Waterloo’s forward outposts.
