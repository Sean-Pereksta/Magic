Gunpowder and war clubs

Use the Infantry, Cavalry, Ranged and Siege tabs in the army builder. Musketeers fire every 12 seconds and stagger the first enemy they hit. Field cannons scatter packed ranks every 22 seconds. Trebuchets lob boulders over walls and need 24 seconds to reload. Select one of these formations to see its loaded weapons. Berserkers carry two-handed clubs that sweep the ground in front of them. Try all four in Thunder at Stonekeep.

Win with the right force

  • Keep spears in the line. Pikemen punish mounted charges. Heavy shields buy your ranged troops time.
  • Use the flanks. Cavalry move quickly and strike hard on a charge. Avoid pushing horses into a spear wall.
  • Give missile troops room. Javelins skirmish close and reposition between throws. Archers form broad shooting ranks at longer range; crossbows plant to aim and reload, favoring armored targets. Give each formation space to fire.
  • Break dense formations. Catapult stones and war elephants deal area damage. Give them a screen of infantry.
  • Read the terrain. Forests slow cavalry and shelter troops from missiles. Hills benefit ranged units. Rivers and rocks block movement; bridges create chokepoints.
  • Arrange your battle line. Use T, then click a direction to face before the advance. Formations keep their marching ranks and approach their local front before spreading into combat. Choose Encircle to give cavalry a route around the wing into the enemy rear, or face and command each wing yourself.
  • Let the ranks bend. Soldiers adjust their local spacing around obstacles, pressure, and nearby allies, then recover their chosen formation in open ground. Passing troops still slow down and use real gaps.
  • Direct their attention. Automatic troops fight enemies already within reach, even from a different formation. Move and Charge keep the ordered direction; Hold defends the current position. Press G to return to automatic battle behavior.
  • Build a broad front. Automatic attacks spread regiments across available enemy frontage. Waiting melee troops seek reachable openings around crowded allies, and passing lanes widen as the approach opens. Use open flanks and keep ranged troops behind your line.
  • Keep formations cohesive. Collision, contact, movement, and flanking disrupt the ranks. Formation bonuses weaken as cohesion falls.
  • Pull a formation out of melee. Press R to start withdrawing on the next simulation step. Soldiers turn while moving and seek friendly support after clearing the fight. Use M and click to choose their destination yourself. The command also works while withdrawing or regrouping. Repeating it keeps the withdrawal moving. Shaken troops can recover and return under a commander.
  • Protect your commanders. Gold standards mark your leaders. Press J to find one, then V to rally nearby shaken and retreating troops. Move commanders behind the formations that need help.

Formations earn their advantage

Change the formation of selected regiments in deployment or during battle. Bonuses scale with cohesion; a disordered group cannot keep a perfect shield wall or charge wedge.

Battle plans that give orders

Open Battle plans to assign an army a strategy before or during battle. The plan directs formations on Auto; your Move, Hold, Face, Charge, and Retreat commands take priority. Press G to return selected formations to the army plan. Steady Advance moves a cohesive infantry front with partial bracing; pikes and hoplites gain the most, and stationary bracing remains stronger. Rapid Encirclement chooses an isolated formation or a local cluster of enemy squads, then assigns a pinning front and fast surrounding wings. Its scope and target count appear on the selected formation. Refused Flank, Concentrated Breakthrough, Elastic Defense, and Echelon Assault give different advance and withdrawal sequences. Select a formation to see its current phase and planned route. Encircle takes cavalry around the enemy wing and into the rear; its turning points stay committed as the formation advances. Narrow terrain or an occupied route can delay or redirect a maneuver.

Build a custom encounter

Choose Custom Map or New empty encounter to clear both armies and all terrain. Choose Standard, Large, Huge, or Colossal battlefield size; the largest field has 6.25 times the original area. Enlarging a field keeps your existing terrain and troops in place. Shrinking requires every formation and terrain area to fit inside the new bounds. Choose Castle and drag its perimeter for four walls, corner towers and a west gate. A click places a square keep. You can also draw individual terrain, wall and gate areas. Choose which army defends new walls and gates. A gate painted across a wall replaces the stone beneath it. Erase and Undo revise the layout. Choose an army, troop type, and size, then Place on field and click a clearing. Select troops and right-click to reposition them. Press T and click a direction to turn their formation. Press B or Split to divide a formation into independently commanded groups. Deployment splits, facing, and custom terrain are included in scenario saves. Give each army troops before starting.

Build momentum, break the line

Horse troops now travel substantially faster than infantry. Lancers are slower than Royal Cavalry and Steppe Outriders, while mounted commanders can keep pace with the cavalry. Cavalry and lancers accelerate into a charge. Give them a straight run-up: speed increases impact damage and displacement. Fast lancers can burst through several levy ranks. Weak light troops are driven backward and outward from the horse's path, opening real gaps; a falling front-rank target no longer ends the follow-through. Light infantry can be thrown into the air; formed hoplites and prepared pikes absorb more force. Pressure passes into troops behind the first rank and fades through the line. Each enemy impact slows the horse, so a thin screen can be pierced while a deep, braced formation can stop the charge. The speed meter shows the selected riders' actual momentum. Sharp turns reduce it; Retreat starts a withdrawal while the riders turn.

Friendly riders make room

Lancers, Royal Cavalry, and Steppe Outriders sidestep to open real gaps for nearby friendly horses. Nearby companies can follow the same opening at riding speed. Their leading horses move first, even when the next rank belongs to a different company. Two lone riders on a collision course can pass one another sideways on open ground. Held horses can take a small sideways step while keeping their facing. Move, Hold, Face, and Retreat replace the previous interaction; turning troops finish their turn first. Enemy ranks, prepared pikes, and solid terrain still resist a charge. Sideways steps do not add impact power.

Give ground under pressure

Sustained close contact lets a stronger force slowly drive an enemy formation and its supporting ranks backward. Bring reserves into physical contact behind your front to add weight. Pressure builds while contact lasts. A regiment can rout after losing at least 80% of its starting strength if its morale also collapses. Troop mass, quality, cohesion, engaged frontage, and supporting ranks determine the pressure; facing, bracing, and firm formations increase resistance. Rear ranks step back with the front while retaining their facing. Rocks and occupied ground can stop the line, and distant separated troops do not get pulled along. Backward and sideways shuffling show the real movement. GIVING GROUND appears when pressure is moving the formation, unless a command or morale status takes priority. Move, Face, and Retreat take control immediately; Retreat immediately starts a breakoff. Hold stops your active advance, but a stronger enemy can still push your line back.

Assault the fortifications

Choose Stonekeep Siege, Floodgate Citadel or The Three Crowns for an enclosed castle. Select a defending formation and right-click or tap a castle gate to open or close it. Either army can use an open gate. The gate stays open while the entrance is occupied. Siege towers roll to enemy walls and take four seconds to deploy a raised assault ramp; infantry then climb through the new opening. Battering rams must physically reach a gate before breaking it. Catapults and ballistas can bombard fortifications, while melee infantry can assault gates. Select an assault formation and right-click an enemy wall or gate to assign it. Auto finds useful siege work; Move, Hold, Face, and Retreat override that job. Towers remain archery platforms after deployment. Narrow bridges, breaches, and ramps require real space, so send a screen ahead and stagger your reserves.

Watch the fight

Troops march, brace, charge, strike, recover, and react to hits with distinct poses. In Settings, choose Brief, Long, or Entire battle for fallen soldiers. Entire battle lets bodies accumulate in visible piles at their death locations until you reset the encounter. Use Reduced animations or Performance detail when viewing especially large fights.
